Asset Sale vs. Stock Sale When You Sell Your HVAC Company

The structure of the deal can change what you keep after tax as much as the price does. Here is how to think about it before you talk to a buyer.

The short answer

In an asset sale, the buyer purchases the company's assets (trucks, tools, customer agreements, goodwill) and generally leaves liabilities behind. In a stock sale, the buyer purchases your shares or membership interests and takes over the entire company, liabilities included. Sellers generally prefer stock sales for tax reasons; buyers generally prefer asset sales. Which one you end up with is negotiated, and it depends on your entity type.

Where it matters for an HVAC company

HVAC businesses carry a lot of depreciated equipment: service vans, trucks, tools and test equipment. In an asset sale, the part of the price allocated to equipment you have already depreciated can be taxed as ordinary income (depreciation recapture) rather than capital gain. Inventory is generally ordinary income either way. Customer lists, maintenance agreements and goodwill are typically a mix. How the purchase price is allocated across those categories is negotiated, so it is worth settling before the final agreement.

Licenses and permits are the practical side. Some contractor licenses, manufacturer dealer agreements and customer contracts do not transfer automatically in an asset sale and need to be re-papered, which is one reason sellers and buyers sometimes agree to a stock sale or an asset-like structure instead.

What to do before you sign a letter of intent

  • Ask your CPA to model your after-tax proceeds under both structures, using your actual entity type and depreciation schedule.
  • Compare offers on after-tax proceeds, not only the headline price. Earnouts, rollover equity and holdbacks change what lands in your account.
  • Ask how the buyer proposes to allocate the purchase price, and push back on allocations that shift value into ordinary income.
  • Have an attorney review the structure and the indemnification terms.

This page is general information, not tax or legal advice. Rules depend on your entity type, state and facts; talk to your own CPA and attorney.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between an asset sale and a stock sale?

In an asset sale the buyer purchases the company's assets (trucks, tools, customer contracts, goodwill) and usually leaves liabilities behind. In a stock sale the buyer purchases the owner's shares or membership interests and takes over the whole company, including its liabilities.

Which is better for the seller of an HVAC company?

Sellers generally prefer a stock sale because the whole gain is typically taxed as capital gain. An asset sale can turn part of the gain into ordinary income, for example depreciation recapture on trucks and equipment, and for a C corporation it can mean two layers of tax. The actual difference depends on your entity type, your depreciation history and how the purchase price is allocated, so have a CPA model it.

Why do buyers prefer an asset sale?

An asset purchase gives the buyer a stepped-up tax basis in what they buy, which can produce larger depreciation and amortization deductions, and it lets the buyer avoid inheriting unknown liabilities. That is why many buyers ask for an asset deal or an asset-like structure.

Can I negotiate the structure instead of just accepting it?

Yes. Structure is a negotiated term, and the price a buyer will pay often moves with it. Compare offers on after-tax proceeds, not just the headline number, and bring your CPA in before you sign a letter of intent.
