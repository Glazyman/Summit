What We Do

The Right Introduction

We don’t broadcast your business to a list. We identify the specific buyers who are actively acquiring in your sector, screen them for financial capability and cultural fit, and make targeted introductions. Every buyer who learns about your business has earned that access.

A Buyer Who Fits, Not Just One Who Pays

The highest offer isn’t always the best outcome. Some buyers want to grow what you’ve built. Others prioritize synergy, rollup, or integration—not necessarily continuity of legacy operations. We take time to understand what matters to you beyond the closing number and bring buyers whose plans for the business line up with yours.

You Keep Running Your Business

Your employees, customers, and competitors should never know a sale is underway until you choose to tell them. We handle every buyer conversation, information request, and disclosure step on your behalf, so the business you’re selling keeps performing like the one they’re buying.

A Deal You Actually Understand

Cash at close, earnouts, rollover equity, asset vs. stock: structure can mean hundreds of thousands in real difference. We walk you through every term before you sign anything, so you know exactly what you’re agreeing to and exactly what you’re walking away with.

Zero Seller Fees. Full Alignment.

Sellers owe Summit nothing upfront or at closing: no retainers, no hourly invoicing of owners, and no deduction from proceeds. Brokerage economics follow customary purchaser-side practice in ordinary closing documentation.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know I'll get a fair price and not be outmaneuvered? +

This is the right question to ask. Buyers do this every week. Most owners sell once. We bridge that gap through process discipline and market knowledge. We know what businesses like yours trade at, we've seen how buyers negotiate, and we know where value gets left on the table. Our job is to make sure that doesn't happen to you.

What happens to my employees after the sale? +

This is the concern we hear most often, and it's a fair one. Not all buyers are the same. Private equity groups typically want your management team to stay; losing key people destroys the value they just paid for. Family offices often say explicitly: 'We're buying your people, not replacing them.' Strategic buyers are more varied. We vet buyer intentions before any disclosure and negotiate employee protections into deal terms when it matters to you. The right buyer sees your team as an asset. We make sure you find that buyer.

What types of businesses do you work with? +

We specialize in profitable service businesses with annual revenues between $2M and $50M: HVAC, access control and security, fencing, professional services, technology, healthcare services, manufacturing, and similar established enterprises. The common thread is consistent cash flow, a real management team, and a business built to run without the owner in every room.

How does deal structure affect what I actually walk away with? +

The headline number is rarely the whole story. Whether the deal is structured as an asset sale or stock sale can mean $500K to $1M or more in tax difference on a $10M transaction. Earnouts are future payments tied to performance targets that can inflate the stated price while reducing what you actually receive. Rollover equity means reinvesting a portion back into the company alongside the buyer, which can be significant upside if they grow the business. We walk you through every structure before you sign anything.

How long does the sale process take? +

Most transactions close within 3 to 6 months from initial engagement. Clean financials and a prepared seller move faster. Businesses with customer concentration issues, owner-dependent operations, or unclear earnings take longer because buyers slow down when they find surprises. The best way to shorten your timeline is to prepare before going to market, which is something we help with from the first conversation.

Can I stay involved in the business after the sale? +

Yes, and many buyers prefer it. Private equity groups often ask founders to stay on for a transition period of 6 to 24 months, sometimes longer in an executive capacity. Family offices and strategic buyers sometimes want the original owner to continue leading the business entirely. We discuss your preferences early and find buyers whose plans align with what you want the next chapter to look like.

What if I'm not ready to sell for a few years? +

Start the conversation now. Owners who get the best outcomes almost always prepared 2 to 3 years in advance: cleaning up financials, reducing owner-dependence, building out management, and understanding what institutional buyers will scrutinize. Many of our strongest client relationships begin well before a transaction. We can tell you what your business is worth today and what it would take to command a premium when you're ready.

Do I pay any fees as the seller? +

No. Sellers pay nothing upfront or at closing, with no withholding from proceeds for Summit fees. Typical practice is customary purchaser-side brokerage documented alongside the broader deal.
