How Much Is a Plumbing Company Worth? Plumbing Business Valuation, Explained for Owners

Buyers price plumbing companies on adjusted earnings and a multiple. Here is how that works, and the plumbing-specific details that move your number up or down.

By Daniel Babayov, Founder of Summit Mergers

Short answer: Most plumbing companies are valued as adjusted earnings times a multiple. Smaller owner-run shops are usually priced on seller’s discretionary earnings (SDE), larger ones on EBITDA. The multiple rises with service and repair revenue, recurring maintenance or commercial service contracts, a master plumber license that stays with the business, a manager who runs the day to day, and clean financials.

The basic formula

Adjusted earnings × multiple = value. A buyer starts from the profit your plumbing company really produces each year, then applies a multiple that reflects how safe and repeatable that profit looks. Debt, cash, working capital and deal terms then decide what you actually take home.

SDE or EBITDA: which one applies to you?

SDE (seller’s discretionary earnings)EBITDA
What it isProfit plus your salary and personal benefits, as if one owner-operator runs the businessEarnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization, after paying a market salary for management
Typically used forSmaller plumbing shops where the owner is still in the trucks or on every estimateLarger plumbing companies with a service manager or GM, and most private equity deals
What it tells a buyerWhat one owner can take homeWhat the business earns without you

Add-backs: finding your real earnings

Buyers "normalize" your books by adding back costs that will not continue after the sale. Common plumbing add-backs:

  • Your pay above what a hired service manager would cost
  • Personal vehicles, phones, travel or family members run through the business
  • One-time costs, such as a lawsuit, a move or a one-off equipment repair
  • Rent paid to yourself above market, if you own the shop or yard

Every add-back needs paper behind it. Buyers test them during diligence, often with an outside accounting review.

Why the license question matters so much

In most states a plumbing company needs a licensed master plumber to pull permits. If that person is you, a buyer has to know how the license will be covered after closing: a licensed employee who stays, a transition period where you remain qualifier, or a buyer who brings their own. An unanswered license question makes buyers nervous, and nervous buyers lower their offers or add earnouts. Sort it out before you go to market. Your attorney can tell you what your state requires.

Rules of thumb and online calculators

“Percent of revenue” rules and online calculators can give a rough starting point, but they can’t see what buyers pay for most: your service mix, your license plan, your team and how clean your books are. Two plumbing companies with the same revenue can sell for very different prices.

Price isn’t what you take home

Two offers with the same headline price can leave you with very different amounts. Cash at closing, earnouts, rollover equity, escrow, working capital targets, and whether it is an asset or stock sale all change your net proceeds. See asset vs. stock sale and how earnouts work.

How to raise your plumbing company’s value in the next 1–3 years

  1. Shift the mix toward service and repair and away from one or two builders.
  2. Put recurring work in writing: maintenance plans and commercial service agreements that transfer to a new owner.
  3. Solve the license question so the business can keep pulling permits without you.
  4. Build a manager under you who runs dispatch and pricing.
  5. Get CPA-prepared financials and separate personal expenses now.
  6. Talk to your CPA about taxes early. Some planning only works years before a sale.

Frequently Asked Questions

Question:How much is a plumbing company worth?

Answer: It depends on your adjusted earnings and the multiple a buyer applies. Smaller owner-run shops are usually priced on SDE and larger ones on EBITDA. Service-heavy revenue, recurring service agreements, a license that stays with the company, a manager under you and clean books all push the price up. Real offers from several buyers are the most reliable measure.

Question:How do you value a plumbing business?

Answer: Buyers normalize your profit with documented add-backs, choose SDE or EBITDA depending on size, then apply a multiple based on revenue mix, recurring work, owner dependence, team stability, customer concentration and financial quality.

Question:Is a service plumbing company worth more than a new-construction plumbing company?

Answer: Usually, yes. Service and repair work tends to repeat and is spread across many customers, while new-construction work depends on a few builders and on the housing cycle. Buyers generally pay more for revenue they expect to continue.

Question:What happens to my plumbing license when I sell?

Answer: That depends on your state and the deal. Common solutions are a licensed employee who stays on, a period where you remain the qualifier after closing, or a buyer who brings their own license holder. Have your attorney confirm what your state requires before you go to market.

Question:How can I increase the value of my plumbing business before selling?

Answer: Grow service and repair revenue, put maintenance and commercial service agreements in writing, solve the license question, build a manager under you, get CPA-prepared financials and talk to your CPA about tax planning a few years before a sale.
