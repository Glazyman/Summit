How Much Is Your HVAC Business Worth? HVAC Business Valuation: How HVAC Companies Are Actually Priced

Buyers price HVAC companies with a simple formula and a long list of details. Here is how SDE, EBITDA and the multiple work, and what moves your number up or down.

Short answer: Most HVAC companies are valued as adjusted earnings times a multiple. Smaller companies are usually priced on seller’s discretionary earnings (SDE), larger ones on EBITDA, and the multiple a buyer applies rises with recurring maintenance-agreement revenue, management depth, technician retention, clean financials and growth.

The basic formula

Adjusted earnings × multiple = value. Buyers start from your real, recurring profit, then apply a multiple that reflects how risky or attractive that profit is. Debt, cash, working capital and deal structure then decide what you actually take home.

SDE vs. EBITDA: which one applies to you?

SDE (seller’s discretionary earnings)EBITDA
What it isProfit plus the owner’s salary and personal benefits, as if one owner-operator runs the businessEarnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization, after paying a market salary for management
Typically used forSmaller, owner-run companiesLarger companies with a management team, and most private equity deals
Why it mattersShows what one owner can take homeShows what the business earns without you

Add-backs: finding your real earnings

Buyers "normalize" your financials by adding back costs that will not continue after the sale. Common HVAC add-backs include:

  • Owner pay above what a hired manager would cost
  • Personal expenses run through the business (vehicles, phones, travel, family on payroll)
  • One-time costs such as a lawsuit, a move or an unusual repair
  • Rent paid to yourself above market rate, if you own the building

Every add-back has to be documented. Buyers will test them during diligence, often with an outside accounting review.

Why HVAC multiples you read online don’t agree

Search for "HVAC EBITDA multiple" and you will find ranges that disagree, sometimes widely. That is because they come from different company sizes, different years, different buyer types and sources with different reasons to quote high or low. A small owner-run shop and a large commercial company with a management team are not priced the same way. Treat any single number as a rough reference, not a promise. The most reliable measure of what your company is worth is real offers from several qualified buyers.

Rules of thumb and online calculators

"Percent of revenue" rules and online valuation calculators can give a starting point, but they ignore the factors buyers care about most, such as your agreement base, your team and how clean your books are. Two companies with the same revenue can sell for very different prices.

Price isn’t what you take home

Two offers with the same headline price can leave you with very different amounts. Cash at closing, earnouts, rollover equity, escrow, and whether it is an asset or stock sale all change your net proceeds. See asset vs. stock sale and selling to private equity.

How to raise your HVAC company’s value in the next 1–3 years

  1. Grow and document your maintenance-agreement base. Written, renewable, transferable agreements.
  2. Build a manager under you so the business runs without you in every decision.
  3. Get CPA-prepared financials and separate personal expenses now, not during the sale.
  4. Keep your technicians with fair pay and a reason to stay.
  5. Reduce customer concentration if one account is a big share of revenue.
  6. Talk to your CPA about taxes early. Some tax planning only works if it is set up years before a sale.

Frequently Asked Questions

Question:How do you value an HVAC business?

Answer: Most HVAC businesses are valued as adjusted earnings times a multiple. Buyers first normalize your profit with documented add-backs, using SDE for smaller owner-run companies or EBITDA for larger ones, then apply a multiple based on recurring revenue, management depth, technician retention, financial quality and growth.

Question:What is a good EBITDA multiple for an HVAC company?

Answer: There is no single number. Published ranges vary widely depending on company size, buyer type, timing and who is quoting them. Recurring maintenance revenue, a management team, low technician turnover and clean financials push the multiple higher. Real offers from several buyers are the most reliable measure.

Question:What is the difference between SDE and EBITDA?

Answer: SDE adds the owner’s salary and personal benefits back to profit and shows what one owner-operator can take home; it is common for smaller companies. EBITDA assumes a market salary for management and shows what the business earns without you; it is common for larger companies and private equity deals.

Question:Can I use an online HVAC business valuation calculator?

Answer: An online calculator can give a rough starting point, but it cannot see the things buyers pay for most, such as your maintenance-agreement base, management team, technicians and the quality of your books. Treat it as a ballpark, not a price.

Question:How can I increase the value of my HVAC business before selling?

Answer: Grow and document recurring maintenance agreements, build a manager under you, get CPA-prepared financials with personal expenses separated, keep your technicians, reduce customer concentration, and talk to your CPA about tax planning a few years before you sell.
