Selling Your Business on Your Own? Do I Need a Business Broker to Sell My Business?

You can sell a business without a broker. Whether you should depends on your company’s size, whether you already have a buyer, and how much time and leverage you have. Here is an honest breakdown.

By Daniel Babayov, Founder of Summit Mergers

Short answer: No, you don’t legally need a business broker to sell your business. Owners who already have a serious buyer and a simple deal sometimes sell on their own with an attorney and CPA. A broker tends to help most when you want several buyers competing, need to keep the sale confidential, or have a larger company with a more complex deal.

What a business broker actually does

  • Finds buyers and gets more than one interested, so you are not negotiating against a single offer.
  • Keeps the sale confidential by screening buyers and using NDAs before your name is shared.
  • Manages the process: buyer questions, document requests and timelines, so you can keep running the business.
  • Helps you compare offers, since cash at closing, earnouts, rollover equity and asset vs. stock structure all change what you take home.

When selling without a broker can work

  • You already have a serious, qualified buyer, such as a key employee, a partner or a competitor you trust.
  • The deal is small and simple.
  • You have time to handle buyer questions and paperwork yourself.
  • You have an experienced M&A attorney and CPA on your side.

When a broker usually helps

  • You want several buyers competing, because one bidder means no leverage.
  • Confidentiality matters, so employees, customers and competitors don’t find out early.
  • Your company is larger, with private equity, family office or strategic buyers likely to be interested.
  • You have never sold a business before and the buyers across the table do it regularly.

Questions to ask any broker

  1. Who pays your fee, how much, and when?
  2. Is there a retainer, minimum fee, or exclusivity period, and how long does it last?
  3. How many companies like mine have you helped sell, and in what industries?
  4. Which buyers will you contact, and how do you keep my name confidential?
  5. Will I see more than one offer?

Red flags

  • Large upfront fees with no clear plan for finding buyers.
  • Promising a specific sale price before seeing your financials.
  • Long exclusive contracts with no way out.
  • Pressure to accept the first offer.

Frequently Asked Questions

Question:Do I need a business broker to sell my business?

Answer: No. There is no legal requirement to use a business broker. Owners with a qualified buyer already lined up and a simple deal sometimes sell on their own with an attorney and CPA. A broker usually helps most when you want several competing buyers, need confidentiality, or have a larger, more complex company.

Question:Can I sell my business without a broker?

Answer: Yes. Selling without a broker can work if you already have a serious buyer, the deal is simple, and you have an experienced M&A attorney and CPA. The trade-off is that you handle buyer questions and paperwork yourself and usually negotiate against a single buyer.

Question:How much does a business broker cost?

Answer: It depends on the type of broker. Sell-side brokers are usually paid by the seller, often through a success fee at closing and sometimes an upfront retainer. Buy-side brokers like Summit Mergers are paid by the buyer, so they cost the owner nothing.

Question:What is the difference between a sell-side and buy-side broker?

Answer: A sell-side broker works for the seller and is usually paid by the seller. A buy-side broker works with buyers who are actively acquiring and introduces them to owners; the buyer pays the fee. If you work with a buy-side broker, have your own attorney and CPA review the terms.
