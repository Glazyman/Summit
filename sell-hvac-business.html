How Is an HVAC Business Valued?

Short answer: HVAC business valuation usually comes down to one number buyers call the "EBITDA multiple." Most established HVAC companies are valued as a multiple of their adjusted earnings (EBITDA, or seller's discretionary earnings for smaller firms). The multiple a buyer will pay depends mainly on how much of the revenue is recurring, how much the business relies on the owner, and how clean and verifiable the books are.

Two HVAC companies with the same revenue can sell for very different prices. The table below shows what moves the number.

What buyers look atWhy it changes your price
Maintenance agreements & service contractsWritten, transferable agreements are predictable revenue. Buyers pay the most for cash flow they can count on after you leave.
Service & replacement vs. new constructionRepeat service and replacement work is steadier than builder bids, which rise and fall with construction cycles.
Owner dependenceIf you run dispatch, sales and the key customer relationships yourself, buyers discount the price for the cost of replacing you.
Technician retentionLicensed, long-tenured techs are hard to hire. Low turnover tells a buyer the business will keep running after the sale.
Clean, verifiable financialsBuyers pay for earnings they can verify. CPA-prepared statements with personal expenses separated hold up in due diligence.
Customer concentrationIf one customer or contract is a large share of revenue, buyers see risk and price it in.

How to Sell Your HVAC Business, Step by Step

  1. Know your numbers: have your CPA pull clean financials and adjusted EBITDA so you can judge offers on your own terms.
  2. Prepare the business: clean up the financials, document your maintenance agreements, and reduce how much depends on you.

  4. Compare offers on structure, not just price: cash at close, earnouts, asset vs. stock sale, and plans for your team.

The 3 Biggest Mistakes HVAC Owners Make When Selling

Each one can cost you hundreds of thousands of dollars, and most owners don't find out until it's too late.

1

Waiting until you're burned out

Buyers pay for where the business is heading, not where it's been. When the owner is tired, it shows up in the numbers: flat growth, techs not replaced, agreements not renewed. Sell from strength, not exhaustion. Use the 12-point checklist to get ready.

2

Taking the first offer that shows up

The first buyer to call is rarely the one who pays the most, or the one who'll take care of your team. With only one buyer at the table, you have no leverage, and they know it.

3

Looking at the price instead of the deal

An asset sale vs. a stock sale can mean $500K to $1M or more in taxes on a $10M deal. Read the asset vs. stock sale guide. Earnouts can make an offer look bigger than what actually lands in your bank account.

Answer: It depends mostly on your adjusted earnings and the quality of those earnings. HVAC companies are usually valued as a multiple of EBITDA or seller's discretionary earnings, and that multiple rises with recurring maintenance-agreement revenue, a management team that runs the business without you, low technician turnover and clean financials.
