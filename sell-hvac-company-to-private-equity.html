HVAC Owners: Got a Private Equity Offer? Selling Your HVAC Company to Private Equity: What to Know Before You Sign

Private equity firms are buying HVAC companies across the country. Here is how they work, what they pay for, and the questions to ask before you agree to anything.

Short answer: Private equity firms buy HVAC companies because the work is essential, the revenue is often recurring, and the industry is made up of thousands of local companies they can combine. A PE sale can pay well and many buyers want your team to stay, but the deal usually comes with rollover equity, reporting and changes after closing, so the terms matter as much as the price.

Why private equity is buying HVAC companies

HVAC has become one of the most actively acquired trades because it has what investors look for in a small business:

  • Essential demand. Systems fail in July and January whether the economy is good or bad.
  • Recurring revenue. Maintenance agreements and commercial service contracts make cash flow predictable.
  • A built-in replacement cycle. Every installed system becomes future service and replacement work.
  • A fragmented market. Most HVAC companies are locally owned, so a buyer can grow by acquiring many of them.

How HVAC "roll-ups" work: platform vs. add-on

Most private equity firms build a group of HVAC companies over several years, usually called a roll-up. Where your company fits changes how the deal works.

What private equity buyers look for in an HVAC company

  • Maintenance-agreement and service revenue that renews every year, ideally in written, transferable agreements.
  • A management layer below the owner, such as a GM or service manager who runs day-to-day operations.
  • Technician retention: licensed people who have been with you for years.
  • Clean, verifiable financials with owner expenses clearly separated. Expect a detailed accounting review during diligence.
  • Growth in revenue and margins over the last few years.
  • No heavy customer concentration, meaning no single customer or contract is a large share of revenue.

How a private equity deal is usually structured

The headline price is rarely paid all in cash at closing. Expect some mix of:

  • Cash at closing: the part you take home on day one, after taxes and debt.
  • Rollover equity: you reinvest part of the price into the buyer's company. It can be worth more later if the group grows and is sold again, but it is not guaranteed and you no longer control it.
  • Earnouts: future payments tied to hitting targets after the sale. They can make an offer look bigger than what you actually receive.
  • Escrow or holdback: part of the price held back for a period to cover claims after closing.
  • Employment or transition agreement: your role, pay and how long you stay.
  • Non-compete: limits on working in HVAC in your area after the sale.

Whether the deal is an asset sale or a stock sale also changes your taxes. Read asset sale vs. stock sale for HVAC owners.

What changes after you sell to private equity

Owners who have sold describe the same pattern: buyers say little will change, but after closing the new owner runs the business its own way. Common changes include more reporting and meetings, new software and systems, a stronger sales process for technicians, and rebranding or new equipment suppliers.

Get promises in writing. If something matters to you, such as keeping your team, your company name or how techs are paid, it belongs in the purchase agreement, not just in conversation. Verbal assurances do not survive the closing.

Questions to ask any private equity buyer

  1. Will my company be a platform or an add-on, and what happens to my brand?
  2. How much of the price is cash at closing, rollover equity and earnout?
  3. What happens to my technicians' pay, schedules and sales expectations?
  4. What role do you want me in, and for how long?
  5. How many HVAC companies have you bought, and can I speak with owners who sold to you?
  6. When do you expect to sell the group, and what happens to my rollover equity then?

Who is happiest selling to private equity?

Owners who are ready to step away, have competing offers to compare, and understand the structure before signing tend to be happiest. Owners who want everything to stay the same after the sale are usually the least satisfied, whoever the buyer is.

Frequently Asked Questions

Question:Do private equity firms buy HVAC companies?

Answer: Yes. Private equity firms are among the most active buyers of HVAC companies, often buying several in a region and combining them into a larger group, usually called a roll-up.

Question:What is rollover equity when selling to private equity?

Answer: Rollover equity means you reinvest part of your sale price into the buyer’s company instead of taking it all in cash. It can be worth more if the group grows and is sold again, but it is not guaranteed and you no longer control the business.

Question:Will private equity keep my employees?

Answer: Many private equity buyers want to keep technicians and managers, because skilled people are hard to replace. But how they are paid and managed often changes after the sale. If protecting your team matters to you, put it in the purchase agreement.

Question:How long do I have to stay after selling to private equity?

Answer: It depends on the deal. Owners of larger platform companies are often asked to stay longer, while add-on deals tend to have a shorter transition. Your role, pay and length of stay should be written into an employment or transition agreement.

Question:Is selling to private equity better than selling to a competitor?

Answer: Not always. Private equity often offers a mix of cash and rollover equity, while a strategic buyer such as a larger HVAC company may offer more cash up front and a shorter transition. The right choice depends on your goals, so it helps to compare offers from both.
